# DIVINIVID — Clean Reference Canon

## Purpose
This document restores the **human-approved source preferences** to the front of the visual process and prevents generated project material from rewriting the user's taste by repetition.

It describes what has been positively selected, what should be extracted from each source family, and where the current source universe still needs research.

## Authority rule
- `CORE` — explicitly high-signal / recurring approved reference family.
- `SUPPORT` — useful for a specific mechanism, not the whole identity.
- `CANDIDATE` — newly proposed source to evaluate in Phase 2; not canon yet.

Generated project boards are not references here unless later promoted by the human owner.

---

# 1. Cinema

## CORE — Macario
Extract:
- natural darkness and localized illumination;
- death/spirituality treated with sobriety rather than fantasy spectacle;
- nature and rural/material reality carrying metaphysical weight;
- silence, shadow and ritual atmosphere;
- supernatural presence that feels matter-of-fact.

Do not extract:
- literal Mexican-folklore motifs merely for decoration;
- sepia/vintage-film filters as shorthand.

## CORE — The Seventh Seal
Extract:
- severe black/white relationships;
- monumental composition and negative space;
- religious/existential symbolism treated intellectually;
- human figures against large landscapes/voids;
- iconic staging with restraint.

Do not extract:
- chess/death iconography as direct Brand motifs;
- generic “arthouse black-and-white” styling.

## CORE — The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari
Extract:
- spatial and geometric distortion as psychological meaning;
- graphic shadow;
- tension through perspective and form;
- environment expressing instability.

Do not extract:
- literal painted-set imitation everywhere;
- cartoonish horror.

## CANDIDATE — The Night of the Hunter
Evaluate for:
- mythic good/evil tension;
- silhouette and negative space;
- expressionist lighting inside a legible narrative world.

## CANDIDATE — Vampyr
Evaluate for:
- dream/reality ambiguity;
- spectral materiality, shadow and optical intervention.

## CANDIDATE — The Color of Pomegranates
Evaluate for:
- saturated sacred color without loss of gravity;
- iconographic tableaux;
- tactile material + abstraction.

## CANDIDATE — Andrei Rublev
Evaluate for:
- faith/art/materiality;
- mud, fire, stone, bells, architecture and ritual as lived matter.

## CANDIDATE — Kwaidan
Evaluate for:
- beauty and supernatural unease coexisting in highly designed color worlds.

## CANDIDATE — Häxan
Evaluate for:
- documentary/classificatory logic coexisting with historical grotesque imagery.

# 2. Painting / engraving

## CORE — Caravaggio / tenebrism
Extract:
- light as semantic revelation;
- bodies emerging selectively from darkness;
- dramatic human physicality;
- sacred significance carried through material flesh and staging.

Do not extract:
- faux-oil-paint filters;
- Baroque costume as default Brand language.

## CORE — Gustave Doré
Extract:
- monumental black;
- density and engraving texture;
- scale contrast between human and architecture/nature;
- sublime/religious/fantastic imagery treated with gravity.

Do not extract:
- turn every output into a Doré engraving;
- ornamental detail without hierarchy.

## CANDIDATE — Matthias Grünewald / Isenheim Altarpiece
Evaluate for:
- bodily suffering and sacred luminosity coexisting inside a liturgical object;
- beauty that does not erase physical disturbance.

## CANDIDATE — Albrecht Dürer / Apocalypse
Evaluate for:
- controlled density;
- angels/monsters/landscape inside rigorous compositions;
- print structure suitable for abstraction into Brand rules.

## CANDIDATE — Hieronymus Bosch
Evaluate for:
- anomaly and monster existing *inside* sacred cosmology rather than outside it;
- hybrid forms and micro-narratives.

## CANDIDATE — Gustave Moreau
Evaluate for:
- ceremonial beauty and disturbing/sacred apparition within the same image.

## CANDIDATE — Francisco de Goya / Black Paintings
Evaluate for:
- psychological darkness;
- economy, void and figure/ground pressure;
- unsettling image without decorative horror.

# 3. Anatomy / body / knowledge

## CORE — historical anatomical engraving
Extract:
- woodcut/engraving/cross-hatching;
- muscle, tendon, bone, spine, cranium, eye and section as knowledge objects;
- body as structure and revelation;
- plates, labels, folios, annotation and classification.

Do not extract:
- glossy 3D anatomy;
- modern medical-brand illustration;
- exposed flesh merely to shock;
- blood-red/gore shorthand.

## CANDIDATE — Andreas Vesalius / De humani corporis fabrica
Evaluate as a primary historical anchor for anatomy-as-knowledge, plate composition and body/landscape relationships.

---

# 4. Illuminated manuscripts / sacred image

## CORE — medieval illuminated manuscripts and codices
Extract:
- **real polychromy**, not beige-only medievalism;
- ultramarine, carmine/vermilion, aged gold and other historically plausible pigments;
- flat sacred figures, borders, initials and narrative fragments;
- imperfect hand-built ornament;
- angels, demons, bestiaries, hybrids, astronomical/cosmological symbols;
- marginalia as a different register from central sacred order.

Do not extract:
- blackletter as automatic medieval shorthand;
- parchment texture on everything;
- faux-aged Pinterest treatment;
- “medieval fantasy” costume design.

## CANDIDATE — Hours of Catherine of Cleves
Evaluate for:
- inventive borders/marginal structures;
- sacred miniatures and intense color;
- repeated system with high local variation.

# 5. Angelology / demonology / bestiary

## CORE — historical angels, demons, beasts and hybrids
Extract:
- strange historical imagination;
- symbolic deformation and hybridity;
- creatures integrated into moral/sacred/cosmological systems;
- tension between reverence and absurdity/unease.

Do not extract:
- modern fantasy concept art;
- videogame boss aesthetics;
- photorealistic AI demons;
- occult-shop iconography.

---

# 6. Grotesque

## CORE — grotesque as symbolic anomaly
The approved sequence is:

> **beautiful → strange → unsettling**

Preferred mechanisms:
- deformation;
- hybridization;
- ambiguous anatomy;
- historical marginalia;
- impossible bodies/proportions;
- sacred or intellectual context containing the anomaly.

Not:
> shocking → bloody → horrific

Grotesque is not a synonym for gore.

---

# 7. Cosmology / diagrammatics

## CORE — historical cosmological/astronomical diagrams
Extract:
- orbits, phases, celestial diagrams, charts and map-like structure;
- geometry as knowledge/mystery interface;
- historical cosmography as a cultural information system.

Do not extract:
- tarot branding;
- generic occult sigils;
- zodiac symbolism without source/meaning.

## SUPPORT — scientific notation / archival classification
Useful for:
- axis;
- section;
- folio;
- labels;
- measurement;
- annotation;
- taxonomy.

Must remain subservient to the visual thesis; it is not permission to become a modern medical/lab identity.

---

# 8. Architecture / landscape / scale

## CORE family
Preferred source qualities:
- isolated churches/religious structures;
- ruins;
- mountain/night landscape;
- dark forest;
- corridors and stone spaces;
- monumental architecture with small human scale;
- silence and permanence.

Extract:
- scale;
- shadow;
- ritual progression;
- void;
- stone/material gravity.

Do not extract:
- fashionable concrete/brutalist renders as a default contemporary look;
- generic gothic architecture merely because it is “dark”.

---

# 9. Expressionism

## CORE family
Caligari is the central explicit reference, but the broader transferable grammar includes:
- distortion;
- high tension;
- shadow as shape;
- fractured or unstable space;
- emotional geometry.

This family is strongest for the later CALIGARI route but may enter KIROGRAF only in controlled doses where it serves a master tension.

---

# 10. Annotation / gestural support

## SUPPORT — Basquiat, narrowly
Extract only:
- body/drawing/mark relationship;
- annotation;
- symbols and imperfect manual energy;
- information written into/over image.

Do not extract:
- a wholesale neo-expressionist/urban identity;
- crown/graffiti clichés;
- color/gesture simply because it feels rebellious.

---

# 11. Material canon

CORE material qualities:
- paper;
- parchment/vellum;
- ink;
- engraving/woodcut;
- historical pigment;
- aged gold / gold leaf logic;
- stone;
- aged metal;
- cloth;
- physical print and plate processes.

Principle:
> **real material logic > vintage filter**

Digital work may emulate or translate material behavior only when the mechanism remains legible.

---

# 12. Color territory

## Base
- ink / deep black / charcoal;
- ivory / parchment / off-white;
- white where needed.

## High-signal historical pigments
- ultramarine;
- carmine / vermilion family;
- aged gold.

## Secondary color
May expand through actual manuscript/pigment research. The identity is **not** restricted to black/ivory/burgundy. Medieval polychromy is an approved part of the source universe.

No final hex values are canon at this stage.

---

# 13. Composition principles

High-signal tendencies:
- cinematic/monumental staging;
- dramatic scale shifts;
- close anatomical crop versus huge negative field;
- asymmetry when it creates tension;
- deliberate layers and annotations;
- dense micro-detail versus large calm zones;
- editorial legibility underneath expressive composition.

Grid is allowed as invisible support. Grid spectacle is not an approved identity goal.

---

# 14. Typography territory

No typeface family is frozen.

Desired source qualities:
- editorial;
- treatise;
- inscription;
- plate/caption/folio systems;
- readable contemporary support.

Avoid:
- generic luxury Didone + tracking as instant “premium”;
- blackletter as instant “medieval”;
- gaming/tech display fonts;
- neutral modernist typography becoming the entire personality.

Typography must ultimately pass licensing, ES/EN coverage, readability and digital/print tests.

---

# 15. Core narrative structures

## SUPPORT / conceptual, not literal motifs
- Book of Job — order, suffering, chaos, restoration;
- Revelation / Apocalypse — celestial order, monster, catastrophe, illumination;
- Temptation of Saint Anthony — discipline versus grotesque invasion;
- Divine Comedy — descent, purification, ascent;
- Paradise Lost — celestial order, beauty of fall, rebellion.

Use as narrative architectures, not as mandatory iconography.

Working sequence:
`REVEAL → DISTURB → RECONCILE`.

# 16. Phase-2 candidate rule

The candidate references listed above are **not automatically promoted** because they fit the thesis. Phase 2 must verify what each uniquely contributes and Phase 3 may reject them.

No more than two source works from one artist/film/campaign should dominate a Reference Atlas board unless the human owner explicitly decides the board is studying that source in depth.
