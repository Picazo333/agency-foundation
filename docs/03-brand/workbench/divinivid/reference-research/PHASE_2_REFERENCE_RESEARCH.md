# DIVINIVID — Phase 2 Expanded Reference Research

## Phase status
**CLOSED / PASS — 2026-09-16**

Phase 2 expands the source universe with real works only. It does not assemble the Phase 3 Reference Atlas and does not authorize project-generated identity/moodboard imagery.

## Research objective
Find source works that add a transferable mechanism to the DIVINIVID thesis:

> **Contrast with elegance the tension and harmony between the divine and the grotesque, darkness and light, showing how apparent opposites can exist inside the same system.**

A source survives only when it contributes a mechanism not already adequately covered by the active source set.

## Evaluation fields
Each surviving source records:
- `SOURCE / DATE / CONTEXT`
- `LANE`
- `WHY IT MATTERS`
- `EXTRACT`
- `DO NOT EXTRACT`
- `MASTER TENSION(S)`
- `ATLAS POTENTIAL`
- `RIGHTS / PROVENANCE NOTE`
- `PHASE-2 STATUS`

Status vocabulary:
- `PROMOTE CANDIDATE` — strong Phase-3 Atlas candidate; still requires human board selection.
- `SUPPORT CANDIDATE` — useful mechanism, but should not dominate a board.
- `HOLD` — relevant but currently redundant or risky.
- `CUT` — does not add enough unique value for the current Atlas.

# Lane 1 — Cinema

## The Night of the Hunter — Charles Laughton, 1955
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- demonstrates eerie beauty and dread inside one coherent black-and-white visual language;
- turns moral opposition into silhouette, scale and light rather than literal graphic symbolism;
- combines fable, spirituality, menace and visual simplicity.

Extract:
- silhouette as moral/psychological structure;
- extreme figure/void relationships;
- lyrical image + threat in the same frame;
- black/white polarity with emotional meaning.

Do not extract:
- preacher/tattoo/river motifs as Brand symbols;
- generic Southern Gothic styling.

Tensions: `LIGHT↔DARKNESS`, `DIVINE↔GROTESQUE`, `ORDER↔ABERRATION`.

Provenance: Criterion Collection editorial/film record; copyrighted film imagery remains reference-only unless separately licensed.

## Vampyr — Carl Theodor Dreyer, 1932
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- gives us an alternative to pure black: muted, watery half-light and perceptual ambiguity;
- demonstrates supernatural unease created through optical logic rather than monster spectacle.

Extract:
- low-contrast spectral atmosphere;
- independent shadows / unstable point of view;
- reality becoming strange through light behavior.

Do not extract:
- generic vampire iconography;
- fog/gauze as a universal filter.

Tensions: `KNOWLEDGE↔MYSTERY`, `LIGHT↔DARKNESS`.

## The Color of Pomegranates — Sergei Parajanov, 1969
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- corrects the false assumption that sacred seriousness must be dark or desaturated;
- uses saturated color, tactile material and iconographic tableaux without losing gravity.

Extract:
- sacred polychromy;
- frontal/tableau composition;
- object/material as symbolic carrier;
- color blocks with ritual weight.

Do not extract:
- Armenian cultural motifs detached from context;
- costume/folk ornament as decorative borrowing.

Tensions: `DIVINE↔GROTESQUE`, `BODY↔TRANSCENDENCE`, `LIGHT↔DARKNESS`.

## Andrei Rublev — Andrei Tarkovsky, 1966
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- brings faith, mud, fire, stone, snow, architecture and craft into one tactile spiritual world;
- useful antidote to polished pseudo-sacred branding.

Extract:
- lived materiality;
- sacredness emerging from labor and matter;
- weather/terrain as emotional structure;
- monumental architecture without luxury coding.

Do not extract:
- medieval-Russian costume as identity motif;
- bleakness for prestige.

Tensions: `BODY↔TRANSCENDENCE`, `KNOWLEDGE↔MYSTERY`, `LIGHT↔DARKNESS`.

## Kwaidan — Masaki Kobayashi, 1965
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- supernatural unease can coexist with meticulously designed color and luminous theatrical space;
- proves that unreality can be elegant rather than chaotic.

Extract:
- designed color worlds;
- controlled surrealism;
- luminous supernatural atmosphere;
- precise theatrical artifice.

Do not extract:
- Japanese folklore motifs outside context;
- obvious ghost-horror shorthand.

Tensions: `BEAUTY↔UNCANNY`, `ORDER↔ABERRATION`, `LIGHT↔DARKNESS`.

## Häxan — Benjamin Christensen, 1922
**Status:** `SUPPORT CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- unusual hybrid of scholarly/classificatory presentation and grotesque historical imagery;
- directly relevant to the possibility of `knowledge system containing anomaly`.

Extract:
- lecture/archive framing;
- classificatory sequencing around disturbing imagery;
- documentary ↔ spectacle friction as a warning and opportunity.

Do not extract:
- sensationalism, torture, nudity or demon spectacle;
- historical claims as reliable scholarship without verification.

Tensions: `KNOWLEDGE↔MYSTERY`, `ORDER↔ABERRATION`.

## Shadows of Forgotten Ancestors — Sergei Parajanov, 1965
**Status:** `HOLD`

Reason: tactile nature + visionary intensity is highly relevant, but overlaps materially with `The Color of Pomegranates` and `Andrei Rublev`. Retain as fallback if Atlas coverage of nature/materiality is weak.

# Lane 2 — Painting / engraving / graphic art

## Isenheim Altarpiece — Matthias Grünewald + Niclaus of Haguenau, 1512–1516
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- perhaps the strongest new source for the core thesis: sacred luminosity and bodily disturbance coexist within a liturgical object built for the sick;
- physical suffering is not outside the sacred system — it is structurally inside it.

Extract:
- sacred image capable of containing difficult flesh;
- radical shifts between closed/open states;
- agony ↔ resurrection as system-level contrast;
- color/light used narratively, not decoratively.

Do not extract:
- crucifixion iconography as Brand content;
- sores/wounds as shock imagery;
- denominational symbolism without purpose.

Tensions: all five master tensions.

Rights/provenance: Musée Unterlinden is the authoritative source; artwork itself is historical/public-domain, but museum photography rights still require source-specific handling.

## Illustrations of the Book of Job — William Blake, 1826
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- integrates divine figures, Satan, monsters, celestial geometry, text and engraved borders inside one disciplined page system;
- relevant to DIVINIVID as both narrative and information architecture.

Extract:
- image + border + text as one semantic system;
- celestial/terrestrial vertical hierarchy;
- border as secondary narrative register;
- monsters contained by a rigorous engraved architecture.

Do not extract:
- Blakean angel anatomy as a house style;
- Biblical quotations as decorative texture.

Tensions: `DIVINE↔GROTESQUE`, `ORDER↔ABERRATION`, `KNOWLEDGE↔MYSTERY`.

## The Four Horsemen / Apocalypse — Albrecht Dürer, 1498
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- extreme visual density remains legible because direction, hierarchy and contrast are engineered;
- demonstrates apocalyptic disorder inside extraordinary compositional control.

Extract:
- directional density;
- hatching as tonal architecture;
- controlled collision of figures;
- print-scale monumentality.

Do not extract:
- literal horsemen/apocalypse motifs;
- engraving texture pasted everywhere.

Tensions: `ORDER↔ABERRATION`, `LIGHT↔DARKNESS`.

## Saint Anthony Tormented by Demons — Martin Schongauer, 1470–74
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- exceptionally concise demonstration of serenity at the center of grotesque assault;
- fantastic hybrid creatures are constructed from observed animal detail, making anomaly feel plausible rather than arbitrary.

Extract:
- calm focal body versus chaotic perimeter;
- hybrid creature construction from believable parts;
- grotesque as pressure surrounding order, not replacing it.

Do not extract:
- demon swarm as a content trope;
- religious martyr scene as required iconography.

Tensions: `DIVINE↔GROTESQUE`, `ORDER↔ABERRATION`.

## Hieronymus Bosch — Saint Anthony / grotesque drawings / visions
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE` with strict limits

Why it matters:
- monsters and absurd hybrids live inside a sacred/moral cosmology rather than outside it;
- micro-narrative can coexist with an overall ordered image.

Extract:
- grotesque micro-events inside a larger system;
- hybridization logic;
- anomaly distributed in local zones.

Do not extract:
- `Bosch-like` AI fantasy;
- saturation of every surface with creatures;
- infernal spectacle as the Brand's dominant identity.

Tensions: `DIVINE↔GROTESQUE`, `ORDER↔ABERRATION`.

## Odilon Redon — noirs / Eye-Balloon / Temptation of Saint Anthony, 1878–1896
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- black can become mystery rather than generic darkness;
- fantastic anatomy emerges from subtle tonal materiality and imagination, not gore.

Extract:
- charcoal/lithographic darkness with internal luminosity;
- isolated impossible form in large atmospheric field;
- ambiguous natural ↔ metaphysical morphology.

Do not extract:
- floating eye as a literal Brand motif;
- Symbolist dream imagery as decorative surrealism.

Tensions: `KNOWLEDGE↔MYSTERY`, `BODY↔TRANSCENDENCE`, `LIGHT↔DARKNESS`.

## Goya — Black Paintings, 1820–23
**Status:** `SUPPORT CANDIDATE`

Unique contribution:
- void, economy and figure/ground pressure; useful for learning how little visual information darkness sometimes needs.

Risk:
- easily pushes DIVINIVID toward despair/horror without counterbalancing illumination.

## Gustave Moreau — Salome with the Head of John the Baptist, ca. 1876
**Status:** `HOLD`

Unique contribution:
- disturbing object rendered as holy apparition.

Reason to hold:
- high ornament/decadent beauty risks pulling the Brand toward decorative Symbolist luxury; Atlas should only use it if the `sacred apparition` mechanism remains underrepresented.

# Lane 3 — Manuscripts / sacred image

## Hours of Catherine of Cleves — ca. 1440
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- 25 full-page miniatures, 132 half-page miniatures and elaborate border systems demonstrate a repeated visual language with high local variation;
- borders are not afterthoughts: they act as an alternate register around sacred centers.

Extract:
- center ↔ margin role separation;
- repeated system with non-repetitive border invention;
- intense sacred color;
- micro-detail around calm focal imagery.

Do not extract:
- imitate specific miniature figures;
- ornamental border everywhere.

Tensions: `ORDER↔ABERRATION`, `DIVINE↔GROTESQUE`.

## Luttrell Psalter — before 1340
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- sacred text/initials coexist with line-endings, foliage, animals and grotesques;
- strong precedent for `Sacred Marginalia` as a system rather than random decoration.

Extract:
- formal center/text hierarchy + playful/strange marginal register;
- controlled color and gold inside page architecture;
- local irregularity without systemic collapse.

Do not extract:
- medieval comedy as a default tone;
- copy specific creatures.

## Aberdeen Bestiary — c. 1200
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- animal imagery, moral/sacred interpretation, lavish gold and evidence of production process connect creature, knowledge and manuscript system;
- excellent for visualizing `classification that remains mythic`.

Extract:
- creature as knowledge object;
- illustration + text + moral system;
- precious color/material paired with taxonomy;
- visible traces of making where useful.

Do not extract:
- animal emblem as automatic logo;
- heraldic/fantasy styling.

## Beatus of Liébana — Apocalypse manuscripts
**Status:** `SUPPORT CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- useful for saturated, flattened apocalyptic imagery and symbolic geometry.

Limit:
- choose specific manuscript pages only during Phase 3 after image/provenance inspection; `Beatus` is a manuscript tradition, not one visual object.

# Lane 5 — Anatomy / body / knowledge systems

## Andreas Vesalius — De humani corporis fabrica / Epitome, 1543
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- authoritative anchor for body-as-knowledge rather than body-as-horror;
- woodcut plate, large folio, initials, landscape/body relations and pedagogical structure.

Extract:
- anatomical section as knowledge;
- figure + label + plate hierarchy;
- cross-hatching and line-density as information;
- body standing as a structured object rather than medical UI icon.

Do not extract:
- contemporary clinical branding;
- exposed tissue for shock;
- faux-Latin/medical jargon.

Rights/provenance: Wellcome Collection copy is marked Public Domain Mark.

# Lane 7 — Architecture / landscape / material

## Bruder Klaus Field Chapel — Peter Zumthor, 2007
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- a charred black interior, monolithic material and a single open oculus turn darkness into a vessel for light rather than an aesthetic surface;
- materially embodies `darkness contains illumination`.

Extract:
- darkness produced by process/material, not UI theme;
- vertical orientation toward light;
- puncture/oculus as revelation;
- rough interior ↔ controlled outer form.

Do not extract:
- concrete brutalism as contemporary shorthand;
- chapel geometry as a direct Brand shape.

Rights: contemporary architecture photography is copyrighted; use as reference only unless licensed.

## Notre-Dame-du-Haut, Ronchamp — Le Corbusier, 1950–55
**Status:** `SUPPORT CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- heavy concrete can become luminous through irregular apertures, painted glass and colored light;
- useful counterexample to `sacred = monochrome`.

Extract:
- thickness punctured by color/light;
- asymmetric apertures with hierarchy;
- synthesis of art/material/light.

Do not extract:
- modernist architecture as identity style;
- chapel silhouette as motif.

## Pierre Soulages — Outrenoir
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE` for material/light research

Why it matters:
- explicit conceptual inversion: black is treated as a means of producing reflected light;
- texture controls illumination without adding another color.

Extract:
- relief/texture as light modulator;
- black as active optical surface, not emptiness;
- material difference inside apparent monochrome.

Do not extract:
- make DIVINIVID monochrome;
- imitate Soulages brushwork as a Brand pattern.

## Existing architecture/landscape core family
Retain isolated religious structures, ruins, dark forest, monumental scale, corridors, stone, void and small-human/large-world relationships. Phase 3 should not become an architecture board; these references are spatial grammar support.

# Lane 8 — Editorial / typography / information design

## Hypnerotomachia Poliphili — Aldus Manutius / Francesco Griffo / anonymous woodcuts, 1499
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE`

Why it matters:
- one of the strongest historical examples for harmonious text-image construction rather than ornamental collage;
- type was designed for the book and woodcuts operate as part of a disciplined page.

Extract:
- text/image equivalence;
- inscription-derived type logic;
- woodcut + typography as one architecture;
- calm margins around complex symbolic content.

Do not extract:
- Renaissance antiquarian motifs as Brand decoration;
- copy Griffo typography literally.

Rights/provenance: Met object and imagery are marked Public Domain/Open Access.

## William Blake — Illustrations of the Book of Job
**Status:** `PROMOTE CANDIDATE` cross-lane

Editorial contribution:
- central image, textual border and secondary pictorial border function as one semantic page;
- excellent model for multi-register information without card-based UI.

## Hours of Catherine of Cleves / Luttrell Psalter
**Status:** `SUPPORT CANDIDATE` cross-lane

Editorial contribution:
- hierarchy between central narrative/text and active margins.

## Kelmscott Chaucer — William Morris / Edward Burne-Jones, 1896

# Cross-lane synthesis

## Strongest new sources for Phase 3
These are not ranked aesthetically; they represent the highest unique-mechanism yield:

- Isenheim Altarpiece — sacred system containing difficult flesh and transformation;
- William Blake, Book of Job — divine/grotesque/text/diagram inside one page architecture;
- Martin Schongauer, Saint Anthony Tormented by Demons — serene center / grotesque perimeter;
- Hours of Catherine of Cleves — sacred center / inventive marginal system / polychromy;
- Aberdeen Bestiary — creature as knowledge object;
- Vesalius — anatomy as knowledge;
- Cellarius — polychrome cosmology as rigorous information;
- Odilon Redon — darkness as imaginative/optical mystery;
- The Night of the Hunter — lyrical beauty and dread through silhouette/light;
- Vampyr — spectral half-light and unstable perception;
- The Color of Pomegranates — saturated sacred materiality;
- Andrei Rublev — lived matter + faith/craft;
- Kwaidan — designed color + supernatural unease;
- Bruder Klaus Field Chapel — material darkness pierced by real light;
- Pierre Soulages / Outrenoir — black producing light through surface;
- Hypnerotomachia Poliphili — text/image/page harmony;
- Bill Viola / The Crossing — duality made temporal.

## Sources intentionally limited
- Bosch — powerful, but must not dominate grotesque language.
- Goya — strong darkness/void, but risks collapsing the Brand toward despair.
- Häxan — useful classification/grotesque hybrid, but ethically/formally sensational and not a reliable historical source by itself.
- Robert Fludd — useful macrocosm/microcosm logic, but high occult-brand contamination risk.
- Moreau — useful apparition logic, but decorative/decadent risk.
- Kelmscott Chaucer — excellent systems craft, but strong stylistic gravity and pastiche risk.

## Redundancy cuts / holds
Phase 2 deliberately avoids adding more references where the mechanism is already saturated. Quantity is not a quality signal. In particular, demonology, generic Gothic architecture, modern dark editorial design and additional tenebrist painting do not need expansion before Phase 3.

# Rights / provenance policy for Phase 3

1. `PUBLIC DOMAIN ARTWORK` does not automatically mean every museum photograph is unrestricted.
2. Prefer source pages that explicitly expose Open Access / Public Domain / CC rights where possible.
3. Contemporary film stills, installations and architecture photography remain `REFERENCE_ONLY` unless separately cleared.
4. Record the institution/source URL and item-specific rights state with every Atlas image.
5. The Atlas is an internal research artifact; any later production use requires a separate rights decision.

High-value low-friction source pools identified:
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art Open Access / Public Domain objects;
- National Gallery of Art Open Access public-domain objects;
- Wellcome Collection Public Domain Mark / CC records;
- Library of Congress digitized collections with item-level Rights & Access notes.
