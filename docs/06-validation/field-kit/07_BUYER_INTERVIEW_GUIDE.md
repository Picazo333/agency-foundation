# 07 — Buyer Interview Guide

> **Instrument type:** interviewer-ready script. **Duration: 40 minutes.**
> **Hypotheses:** H-10 (primary), H-06, H-11, H-04, H-03b, H-07.
> **Gate:** D4. **Record into:** `08_INTERVIEW_RECORD_TEMPLATE.md` within 24 hours.
>
> Written so a **second operator** could run it identically. Timings are not suggestions — the
> 12-minute open-narrative phase is the measurement instrument for H-10, and shortening it destroys
> the gate.

## 0. The sealed-phase rule *(the single most important rule in this kit)*

> **Phases 1–3 are SEALED. You may not describe the problem, the offer, the category, the price, or
> any part of the plan's framing until Phase 4 begins.**

H-10 asks whether buyers volunteer the revenue-leak problem **before it is described to them**. The
instant you describe it, that buyer can no longer produce H-10 evidence. There is no way to recover
it, and agreement obtained after framing is worth approximately nothing.

At the top of the record you will write **the exact minute at which you first named the problem.**
If that minute falls inside Phases 1–3, the interview is marked `resonance_contaminated = Y` and
excluded from the H-10 denominator. This is a self-reported field and the whole gate depends on
reporting it honestly.

## 1. Before the call

- [ ] Account research open (`03_ACCOUNT_RESEARCH_TEMPLATE.md`)
- [ ] Interview record open and ready to type into
- [ ] Recording set up (with permission)
- [ ] Timer visible — **you will lose track of the 12 minutes otherwise**
- [ ] **No agenda sent in advance that names the problem** (contaminates H-10)
- [ ] You have decided in advance whether this call may reach Phase 6 (price). Default: no

## 2. Phase 0 — Opening and consent *(2 min)*

> "Thanks for the time. Quick context so you know what this is: I'm mapping how [segment] groups
> handle enquiries and bookings — what actually happens day to day. I'm not here to pitch you
> anything today. I'll mostly be asking questions and listening.
>
> Two things: is it alright if I record this so I don't have to take notes while you talk? It's
> only for my own notes — I won't share it or quote you anywhere without asking you first.
>
> And if I ask something you'd rather not answer, just say so and we'll move on."

- Consent granted / refused → record either way. **If refused, do not record.** Take verbatim notes.
- If they ask "so what do you sell?" → *"Happy to explain at the end — I'd rather not colour your
  answers first. Is that alright?"* Almost everyone accepts this, and it protects the gate.

## 3. Phase 1 — Context *(5 min)*

Factual grounding. Not yet the measurement.

1. "Tell me about the business — how many locations, roughly how many people?"
2. "What's your role day to day?"
3. "How long have you been doing it?"
4. "Who else is involved in decisions about how the front end of the business runs?" *(H-03b)*

**Do not** react to anything as if it were a problem. Stay neutral.

## 4. Phase 2 — Open narrative *(12 min — SEALED, the H-10 measurement)*

**Ask the opener. Then stop talking.**

> **"Walk me through what happens when somebody gets in touch with you for the first time.
> From the very beginning — wherever that starts."**

Then: silence. Let them talk. Twelve minutes.

**Permitted interventions only:**

| Permitted | |
|---|---|
| "Then what happens?" | |
| "Who does that?" | |
| "Can you give me an example of a time that went wrong?" | |
| "What happens if [factual condition they already mentioned]?" | e.g. "…if it arrives at 9pm?" — only if *they* mentioned hours |
| "Say more about that." | |
| Silence | The most productive tool available |

**Prohibited in this phase — every one of these invalidates H-10:**

| Prohibited | Why |
|---|---|
| "Do you lose enquiries?" | Names the problem. Gate over |
| "Is follow-up an issue?" | Names the problem |
| "How much do you think that costs you?" | Names the problem *and* asks them to invent a number |
| "A lot of clinics struggle with X — do you?" | Names it and adds social proof pressure |
| "That sounds like it's leaking revenue" | Names it in the plan's own framing |
| Any mention of what you sell | Ends the seal |
| Filling silence with your own hypothesis | The most common failure |

**What to capture, verbatim and in their words:**
- Every problem they raise **unprompted** → `10_PROBLEM_RESONANCE_LOG.md` as `VOLUNTEERED`
- The exact language they use for it — this becomes the messaging corpus
  (`POSITIONING_ARCHITECTURE.md` §3) and replaces all synthetic research VoC (`E-05`)
- **What they never mention.** An expected pain that never comes up is data

> **The hardest discipline in this kit:** when they describe something that is obviously the
> problem you think exists, do not confirm it, do not name it, do not look pleased. Ask "then what
> happens?" and keep going.

## 5. Phase 3 — Mechanics *(10 min — still SEALED)*

Factual, answerable, non-leading. Still no framing.

1. "Which channels do enquiries actually come through? Roughly what mix?"
2. "Who sees them first? What happens on a normal Tuesday?"
3. "What happens outside opening hours?"
4. "How does it work across your different locations — same process everywhere?"
5. "What happens when somebody enquires and then goes quiet?"
6. "How do people usually book — phone, online, in person?"
7. "What happens when somebody doesn't turn up?"
8. "What do you use to keep track of all this?"

**Measurement sub-block — H-06** *(be exact; this decides whether the diagnostic is sellable)*:

9. "If you wanted to know how many enquiries you got last month, could you find out? How?"
10. "How would you tell which of those turned into appointments?"
11. "How far back does that go?"
12. "Does anyone look at those numbers? How often?"

Record for H-06: does a reconstructable history of **≥4 weeks** exist, and through what system?

**Competitive sub-block — H-07:**

13. "Has anyone else looked at this side of the business for you — agency, consultant, software?"
14. *(if yes)* "What did they do? How did it go?"

## 6. Phase 4 — Consequence and prior attempts *(5 min — seal lifts here)*

The seal lifts **only** because Phase 2 is complete and recorded. Note the minute.

1. "Of everything you've described, what actually bothers you most?"
2. "What happens as a result of that?"
3. "Have you tried to fix it? What happened?"
4. "If it stayed exactly as it is for another year, what would that mean?"
5. "Is it on anyone's list to do something about, or is it just how it is?"

**Note the asymmetry:** question 1 asks *them* to nominate what matters. It does not offer a
candidate. If they nominate something that has nothing to do with the thesis, **that is the finding**
— record it in the resonance log as `OTHER PROBLEM (unanticipated)` and treat it as the most
valuable output of the interview.

## 7. Phase 5 — Authority, process, category language *(3 min)*

**Authority (H-03b, Q3):**
1. "If you decided to do something about this, how would that actually happen? Who signs it off?"
2. "Who else would need to agree, and does that change above a certain spend?" *(multi-owner partnerships — `AUD-05-02`)*
3. "How quickly do decisions like that usually move?"

**Category language (H-11)** — ask exactly this way:
4. "If you were going to go looking for someone to help with what you've described — what would you
   even search for? What would you call them?"
5. "Where would that come from, budget-wise? Is there a line it fits, or would it be ad hoc?"

> Q5 matters more than Q4. A category that maps to no budget line cannot be bought
> (`POSITIONING_ARCHITECTURE.md` §2.1). Record the **budget line** verbatim.

## 8. Phase 6 — Diagnostic transition *(conditional, 3 min)*

**Only enter Phase 6 if all three are true:**
1. They volunteered a problem in Phase 2 or nominated one in Phase 4, **and**
2. They are plausibly in scope (`ICP_FRAMEWORK.md` §6.7), **and**
3. You have already completed Phases 2–5. Never skip forward to sell.

Neutral transition — no enthusiasm, no pressure:

> "That's really useful, thank you. Can I tell you what I actually do, briefly, and you can tell me
> whether it's relevant or not?
>
> I measure exactly what you've just described. I instrument the path from enquiry to booking,
> measure it for a couple of weeks against your real data, and then tell you where things are
> dropping and what each gap looks like in numbers. You keep the measurement either way. If you
> want me to fix anything afterwards, I quote that separately — but plenty of people just take the
> findings.
>
> Is that something that would be useful here, or not really?"

**Then stop and let them answer.** Do not fill the silence.

- If they engage → proceed to the price probe in `11_PRICE_SIGNAL_LOG.md` §3 **only if** the
  qualification scorecard would plausibly return `QUALIFIED` or `QUALIFIED_WITH_RISK`. Otherwise do
  not quote.
- If lukewarm → **do not push.** Log it. A lukewarm response to a clearly explained offer is
  high-quality evidence and pushing destroys it.
- **Never** discount to force engagement (`DNS-13`, H-01 confound).

## 9. Phase 7 — Closing *(2 min)*

> "That's everything I wanted to ask — thank you, genuinely. Two last things:
>
> Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
>
> And is there anyone else in [segment] you think I should be talking to? I'm trying to get to
> about fifteen of these conversations."

Then:

> "I'll write this up in the next day or so. If I want to quote anything you said, even
> anonymously, I'll check with you first. Thanks again."

- "What should I have asked?" regularly produces the single best line of the interview.
- The referral ask is CH-2 and costs nothing.
- The quote-consent promise is binding (`EC-8`, `DR-8`).

## 10. PROHIBITED LEADING QUESTIONS — with neutral alternatives

| # | Prohibited | Why it fails | Neutral alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Do you lose enquiries?" | Names the problem; invalidates H-10 | "Walk me through what happens when someone gets in touch." |
| 2 | "Is follow-up a problem for you?" | Names it; invites agreement | "What happens when somebody enquires and then goes quiet?" |
| 3 | "How much revenue do you think you're losing?" | Names it *and* asks for an invented number | "Could you find out how many enquiries you got last month?" |
| 4 | "Would you pay for something that fixed this?" | Hypothetical purchase intent is unreliable | Quote a real price and observe the reaction (`11_PRICE_SIGNAL_LOG.md`) |
| 5 | "A lot of clinics struggle with X — do you?" | Social proof pressure + naming | "What's the part of this that bothers you most?" |
| 6 | "Don't you think that's inefficient?" | Asks them to agree with your judgement | "What happens next after that?" |
| 7 | "Wouldn't it be useful to have that measured?" | Leading, and pre-sells the offer | "Does anyone look at those numbers? How often?" |
| 8 | "Is your no-show rate high?" | Assumes a problem and a benchmark that does not exist | "What happens when somebody doesn't turn up?" |
| 9 | "Are you happy with your current agency?" | Invites disloyalty; creates the blocker (`PP-6`) | "Has anyone else looked at this side of the business for you?" |
| 10 | "Would this be worth £X to you?" | Hypothetical WTP is worthless | Quote once, then silence |
| 11 | "So the real problem is X, right?" | Puts your framing in their mouth | "What would you call what you've just described?" |
| 12 | "That must be costing you a fortune." | Fabricates economics; invites agreement | "What happens as a result of that?" |
| 13 | "Most people in your position tell me…" | Manufactured consensus; also usually false early on | Say nothing. Let the silence work |
| 14 | "Do you have a budget for this?" *(in Phase 2–3)* | Premature; contaminates the sealed phase | Phase 5 Q5: "Where would that come from, budget-wise?" |

**General test:** *would I be disappointed by one of the possible answers?* If yes, the question is
leading. Rewrite it until every answer is equally interesting.

## 11. Second-operator consistency

For a second interviewer to produce comparable records:

| Requirement | |
|---|---|
| Phase timings are held within ±2 minutes | The 12-minute seal especially |
| Phase 2 opener is asked **verbatim** | It is the instrument |
| The prohibited list in §10 is read before the first three interviews | — |
| Resonance classification is made **from the recording/transcript**, not from memory | `10_PROBLEM_RESONANCE_LOG.md` §3 |
| The contamination minute is recorded on every interview | Non-negotiable |
| Records are completed within 24 hours | Verbatim quality collapses after that |
| The first interview by any new operator is marked `calibration = Y` and excluded from gate counts | Protects the gate from learning-curve noise |

## 12. Post-interview, within 24 hours

1. Complete `08_INTERVIEW_RECORD_TEMPLATE.md` — verbatim section first, before any interpretation.
2. Classify resonance in `10_PROBLEM_RESONANCE_LOG.md` **from the transcript**.
3. If a price was quoted → `11_PRICE_SIGNAL_LOG.md`.
4. If the diagnostic was offered → `12_DIAGNOSTIC_SALES_ATTEMPT_LOG.md`.
5. Run `09_QUALIFICATION_SCORECARD.md`. Update the ledger.
6. Add verbatim phrases to the messaging corpus.
7. Update `../hypotheses/HYPOTHESIS_REGISTER.md` evidence column.
