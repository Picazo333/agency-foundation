## Evidence discipline

Maintain these distinctions throughout:

- `VERIFIED FACT`
- `OBSERVED SIGNAL`
- `INFERENCE`
- `HYPOTHESIS`
- `UNKNOWN`

For financial or pricing claims, distinguish:

- `PUBLIC PRICE`
- `PRICE SIGNAL`
- `INFERRED RANGE`
- `UNKNOWN`

Do not preserve false precision from previous research. If a number cannot be defended, downgrade it to a hypothesis or range.

## Required sequence

### 1. Strategy Reconciliation

Compare at minimum:

- classic agency;
- productized boutique;
- hybrid creative-systems studio;
- consulting-led model;
- implementation-led AI/automation model;
- any stronger model discovered from the evidence.

For each, analyze:

- buyer clarity;
- willingness/ability to pay;
- differentiation;
- delivery complexity;
- repeatability;
- recurring potential;
- proof requirements;
- acquisition difficulty;
- founder dependence;
- margin sensitivity;
- scalability;
- commoditization risk;
- brand fit as a secondary criterion only.

Do not select a winner merely because it fits the current aesthetic.

### 2. Agency Thesis / Category

Define candidate agency theses and category language.

Explain what each model is, what it is not, and what evidence would falsify it.

### 3. ICP Framework

Evaluate candidate ICPs using explicit criteria:

- pain urgency;
- budget;
- decision maker clarity;
- access/channel availability;
- sales cycle;
- digital maturity;
- automation opportunity;
- brand/web pain;
- ROI measurability;
- repeatability;
- recurring potential;
- compliance burden;
- proofability;
- expansion potential.

Healthcare, dental, clinics, labs, professional services, B2B services and other research-backed segments may be considered, but none are predetermined winners.

### 4. Positioning / White Space

Map:

- category claims;
- commoditized promises;
- generic AI language;
- defendable differentiation;
- positioning options;
- what not to claim.

### 5. Offer Architecture

Design alternative offer ladders:

`ENTRY -> CORE -> EXPANSION -> RECURRING`

Include:

- audit / diagnostic;
- strategy sprint;
- web / landing / conversion work;
- automation / AI implementation;
- internal tools;
- creative/brand work if commercially justified;
- maintenance / optimization;
- retainers / managed services;
- what should explicitly not be sold.

### 6. Pricing and Economics

Build models, not invented facts.

For each viable offer architecture, expose:

- price assumptions;
- delivery hours;
- tooling;
- subcontracting;
- revision risk;
- concurrency;
- founder utilization;
- gross-margin sensitivity;
- recurring load;
- capacity constraints.

### 7. Proof Strategy

Define how a new agency can establish trust before having a large client roster.

Include legitimate use of:

- pilot projects;
- internal projects;
- prototypes;
- demonstrations;
- case-study architecture;
- before/after evidence;
- quantified outcomes only where verified.

### 8. Acquisition and Sales System

Design candidate motions:

- founder-led content;
- outbound;
- referrals;
- partnerships;
- lead magnets;
- audit offers;
- discovery;
- qualification;
- proposal;
- follow-up;
- nurturing.

Map:

`TRAFFIC/OUTREACH -> LANDING/CONVERSATION -> PROOF -> QUALIFICATION -> DISCOVERY -> PROPOSAL -> CLOSE`

### 9. Delivery Operating System

Define a practical end-to-end delivery architecture:

- discovery;
- strategy;
- design;
- development;
- automation;
- QA;
- revision control;
- client communication;
- handoff;
- maintenance;
- recurring optimization;
- proof capture.

### 10. Field Validation Plan

This is mandatory.

Specify:

- hypotheses that must be tested outside desk research;
- who should be interviewed/contacted;
- minimum useful evidence;
- willingness-to-pay questions;
- buying triggers;
- objections;
- kill criteria;
- what result should cause the team to mutate or abandon a model.

### 11. 90-Day Roadmap

Build a practical 90-day execution roadmap with dependencies and parallel workstreams.

Do not require final Brand V1 to begin commercial validation where it is unnecessary.
